# BTLPT Spanish (190) — AI Question-Generation Guide
## Style, Task Structure, Topic Patterns, and Generation Rules

**Source:** Texas Education Agency (TEA), *Bilingual Target Language Proficiency Test (BTLPT) — Spanish (190), Preparation Manual*, 2022.

> **Purpose of this document:** This is a generalized question-generation guide derived from the preparation manual. It describes the **style, structure, task types, skills assessed, recurring contexts/topics, and response expectations** without reproducing the manual's sample questions verbatim. It is intended to be used as a reference when prompting an AI to generate new BTLPT-style practice material.

---

## 1. Exam at a Glance

The BTLPT Spanish exam is organized into four domains:

| Domain | Approx. % of exam | Core skill |
|---|---:|---|
| I. Listening Comprehension | 21% | Understand, infer, interpret, and evaluate spoken Spanish |
| II. Reading Comprehension | 26% | Understand, infer, interpret, and critically analyze written Spanish |
| III. Oral Expression | 29% | Produce effective interpersonal and presentational spoken Spanish |
| IV. Written Expression | 24% | Produce effective interpersonal and presentational written Spanish |

The manual states that the test contains **84 selected-response questions and 7 constructed-response tasks**. The four domains correspond to Standards I–IV.

### The central theme of the exam

The exam is not simply a general Spanish-language test. The manual repeatedly places language use in contexts relevant to:

- bilingual classrooms
- students and student needs
- teachers and colleagues
- parents/guardians
- school administrators
- lesson planning
- instructional materials
- content-area teaching
- professional communication
- the school community
- culturally appropriate communication
- academic/TEKS-related vocabulary

Therefore, an AI-generated practice question should normally feel like something an entry-level bilingual educator in a Texas public-school setting could realistically encounter.

**Source basis:** pp. 2–5 of the manual.

# 2. Domain I — Listening Comprehension

## 2.1 What the listening domain measures

The manual defines the listening competency around three levels of comprehension:

1. **Literal** — identify information explicitly stated.
2. **Inferential** — determine information that is implied or logically supported.
3. **Interpretive** — understand tone, mood, point of view, social context, purpose, and culturally relevant meaning.

The listening material should be authentic-sounding spoken Spanish relevant to bilingual education and the school environment.

The manual specifically gives examples such as:

- a parent/guardian conference
- a colleague describing a lesson plan
- a short lecture about a content-area topic
- a telephone message from a parent/guardian
- oral instructions
- professional/social interactions involving students, parents, or educators

The speaker may use academic vocabulary associated with teaching and Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS) content areas.

---

## 2.2 Actual listening format/style

The manual's sample format uses:

- an **audio selection in Spanish**
- the selection is played **twice**
- the candidate previews four questions before the second playback
- **four selected-response questions** are associated with each listening selection
- the questions are presented one at a time
- the candidate chooses the best answer based on the recording
- sample selections vary in style and length

The manual's examples include:

### A. Interpersonal/professional dialogue

A realistic conversation between people connected to a school.

Typical participants:
- teacher + parent
- teacher + administrator
- teacher + colleague
- teacher + student
- school employee + parent
- other school/community participants

Typical characteristics:
- natural conversational turns
- questions and responses
- a practical problem or situation
- implicit information
- changes in tone or attitude
- a final request, decision, or likely next step

### B. Narrative / cultural account

A short story, legend, historical/cultural account, or narrative.

Typical characteristics:
- chronological sequence
- events and motivations
- cultural references
- descriptions of people, places, traditions, or practices
- a final outcome or explanation

### C. Informational / expository report

A short spoken report or mini-lecture.

Typical characteristics:
- an identifiable topic
- explanation of a process, issue, phenomenon, or problem
- facts/examples
- cause-and-effect relationships
- statistics or percentages may be used
- expert/reference attribution may appear
- broader implications or consequences

---

## 2.3 Listening question styles

A generated listening set should usually contain a mixture of question types rather than four questions that all test the same thing.

### Type 1 — Speaker/participant identification

Ask who is speaking or what their relationship is.

Examples of the *style*:
- Who is participating in the conversation?
- What is the relationship between the speakers?
- Which professional role does one speaker occupy?

**Skill:** literal/contextual comprehension.

---

### Type 2 — Setting/context

Ask where the conversation is taking place or what situation it represents.

Possible contexts:
- classroom
- school office
- staff meeting
- parent conference
- school event
- professional development
- community/school setting

**Skill:** infer context from details.

---

### Type 3 — Main idea/topic

Ask what the selection is mainly about.

Good distractors should be:
- related to something mentioned
- narrower than the actual main idea
- a secondary detail
- a plausible but unsupported interpretation

**Skill:** global comprehension.

---

### Type 4 — Explicit detail

Ask about a fact directly stated in the audio.

Examples:
- What did the speaker say?
- What did the teacher recommend?
- What happened first?
- What reason was given?

**Skill:** literal comprehension.

---

### Type 5 — Inference

Ask what can reasonably be inferred.

Useful stems:
- What is most likely to happen next?
- What can be inferred about...?
- What was the speaker probably trying to accomplish?
- Why did the person most likely...?

The correct answer should be **strongly supported by the audio**, not based on outside knowledge.

---

### Type 6 — Tone / attitude / point of view

Ask how a speaker feels or what attitude is conveyed.

Possible tone categories:
- enthusiastic
- concerned
- skeptical
- encouraging
- frustrated
- appreciative
- formal
- optimistic
- cautious

The audio should contain enough linguistic/contextual evidence to support the answer.

---

### Type 7 — Purpose/function

Ask why a speaker said something or what function a portion of the discourse serves.

Possible functions:
- explain
- persuade
- request
- reassure
- inform
- recommend
- clarify
- warn
- emphasize

---

### Type 8 — Consequence/implication

Ask what an event or issue implies for:
- students
- teachers
- families
- a school program
- a content area
- the community

---

### Type 9 — Organization/evidence

Especially for informational reports, ask how the speaker develops the point.

Possible answers:
- gives concrete examples
- provides statistics
- describes a sequence
- compares situations
- cites an expert
- explains cause and effect

---

### Type 10 — Cultural/social interpretation

The manual explicitly includes cultural knowledge and sensitivity.

Generated questions can therefore test:
- appropriateness of a social exchange
- meaning implied by a culturally situated interaction
- speaker relationship/register
- culturally relevant practices or traditions

Avoid requiring obscure cultural trivia. The answer should be supported by the selection.

## 2.5 Listening generation template

Use this structure when asking an AI to generate a practice set:

> **Generate one BTLPT Spanish Listening Comprehension selection.**
>
> - Format: authentic-sounding spoken Spanish.
> - Context: [school/professional/cultural/informational].
> - Length: approximately [X] minutes when spoken naturally.
> - Audience: bilingual educator preparing for the BTLPT.
> - Include enough information to support literal, inferential, and interpretive questions.
> - Include realistic details, but do not make the answer obvious through repetition.
> - If it is a dialogue, use 2–3 clearly distinguishable speakers.
> - If it is informational, develop one coherent topic with examples/evidence.
> - Then generate 4 multiple-choice questions:
>   1. one literal/detail question,
>   2. one main-idea/context question,
>   3. one inference question,
>   4. one interpretive/purpose/tone/evidence question.
> - Provide 4 plausible options per question.
> - Only one option should be clearly best based on the audio.
> - Distractors must be plausible and related to the audio.
> - Do not require outside knowledge.
> - Keep the Spanish natural and professionally appropriate.

# 3. Domain II — Reading Comprehension

## 3.1 What the reading domain measures

The reading competency includes:

- literal comprehension
- inference
- interpretation
- critical reading
- understanding authentic written materials
- understanding academic/content-area vocabulary
- recognizing cultural aspects of the target language

The manual emphasizes texts relevant to both:

1. the bilingual classroom/school environment, and
2. broader TEKS-related academic/content areas.

---

## 3.2 Actual reading format/style

The sample reading section contains several written selections, each followed by multiple selected-response questions.

The manual describes the materials as **authentic written materials** rather than artificial grammar exercises.

Possible text genres include:

- educational articles
- informational articles
- biographies
- cultural/historical texts
- explanatory texts
- school-related materials
- content-area texts

Some selections may include **numbered words with footnotes** providing definitions or explanations.

The reading questions are in Spanish.

---

## 3.3 Reading question styles

### Type 1 — Explicit detail

Ask for information directly stated.

Typical stems:
- Según el texto...
- De acuerdo con el artículo...
- ¿Qué afirma el texto sobre...?
- ¿Cuál de las siguientes ideas se presenta...?

---

### Type 2 — Main idea / central theme

Ask what the passage is mainly about.

Possible stems:
- ¿Cuál es el tema central...?
- ¿Cuál es la idea principal...?
- ¿De qué trata principalmente el texto?

The correct answer should represent the passage as a whole rather than one isolated detail.

---

### Type 3 — Supporting idea

Ask which statement is supported by the passage.

Distractors may:
- reverse the relationship
- exaggerate the author's claim
- introduce an idea from another part of the text
- use a related but unsupported statement

---

### Type 4 — Inference

Ask what can reasonably be concluded.

Examples:
- What can be inferred about the person/topic?
- What is most likely the reason...?
- What does the information suggest?

The inference must be grounded in textual evidence.

---

### Type 5 — Author's viewpoint

Ask what the author would agree with.

This requires combining information across the text rather than locating one identical sentence.

---

### Type 6 — Tone

Possible tone categories:
- didactic/instructional
- critical
- informative
- reflective
- appreciative
- analytical
- concerned
- persuasive

Do not make tone dependent on one adjective alone; the text should establish a consistent attitude.

---

### Type 7 — Purpose

Ask why the text was written.

Possible purposes:
- inform
- explain
- describe
- persuade
- educate
- present a biography
- analyze an issue
- provide guidance

---

### Type 8 — Genre/publication/source

Ask where the text would most likely appear.

Possible source types:
- educational magazine
- school publication
- newspaper
- informational website
- professional publication
- community publication
- cultural magazine

The source should be inferable from style/content.

---

### Type 9 — Author's claim / supported statement

Ask which statement agrees with the content.

This is a close-reading task: the answer must accurately reflect what the text says.

---

### Type 10 — Vocabulary in context

A word or expression can be tested through:
- contextual meaning
- synonym-like interpretation
- meaning in the sentence

If footnotes are used, they can clarify specialized vocabulary without turning the task into a dictionary test.

---

### Type 11 — Organization / rhetorical method

Ask how the author develops an idea.

Possible answers:
- examples
- chronology
- comparison
- cause/effect
- description
- explanation
- evidence
- anecdote

---

## 3.4 Reading topic bank

The manual's samples demonstrate that topics can extend beyond classroom logistics.

Useful categories include:

### Education

### Biography/history

---

## 3.5 Reading generation template

> **Generate a BTLPT Spanish Reading Comprehension practice passage.**
>
> - Write an authentic-sounding Spanish text appropriate for an educated adult bilingual educator.
> - Genre: [educational article / biography / cultural article / informational report / content-area article].
> - Topic: [topic].
> - Make the text coherent and substantial enough to support multiple questions.
> - Include explicit information, relationships that require inference, and enough authorial framing to support tone/purpose questions.
> - Use natural Spanish and appropriate academic vocabulary.
> - Avoid testing obscure facts unrelated to the passage.
> - Generate 8 multiple-choice questions covering a mixture of:
>   - explicit detail,
>   - supporting idea,
>   - main idea,
>   - inference,
>   - author's viewpoint,
>   - tone,
>   - purpose,
>   - genre/source,
>   - vocabulary in context,
>   - rhetorical organization.
> - Do not make all questions literal.
> - Ensure every correct answer is supported by the passage.
> - Provide 4 plausible options per question and one best answer.
> - Do not reuse wording from the TEA sample questions.

# 4. Domain III — Oral Expression

## 4.1 What the oral domain measures

The oral domain evaluates the ability to construct effective spoken discourse for different:

- audiences
- purposes
- occasions
- classroom situations
- professional situations

The manual specifically emphasizes:

- explaining
- narrating
- describing
- using appropriate verb tenses
- formal and informal registers
- general and content-area vocabulary
- communication with students, colleagues, and parents/guardians
- culturally appropriate communication
- responding to everyday classroom situations
- discussing advantages/disadvantages
- expressing an opinion
- proposing a course of action

---

## 4.2 The five oral task types

The manual's directions identify five oral assignments:

1. **Simulated Conversation**
2. **Question and Answer — Part 1**
3. **Question and Answer — Part 2**
4. **Oral Presentation**
5. **Support a Situation/Opinion**

The samples show these as distinct speaking demands even though the Q&A portions are grouped in the sample section.

---

## 4.3 Task 1 — Simulated Conversation

### Style

The candidate enters a realistic conversation with another person.

The manual's sample uses a professional school setting and a conversation with a school administrator.

The screen gives an outline indicating:
- what the other speaker will do/say
- what the candidate is expected to accomplish in response

The candidate has several short turns.

### Typical response characteristics

Each response should:
- directly answer the current prompt
- continue the conversation naturally
- provide requested details
- use the appropriate register
- sound spontaneous rather than like a memorized essay
- maintain professional/culturally appropriate interaction

### Good AI-generated scenarios

- job fair / interview
- meeting with a principal
- conversation with a parent
- discussion with a colleague
- speaking with a student
- discussing a classroom activity
- school event planning
- family engagement
- resolving a routine classroom issue

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Simulated Conversation**.
>
> Context: [school/professional context].
> Candidate role: [teacher/bilingual educator].
> Conversation partner: [parent/principal/colleague/student/etc.].
>
> Give the candidate 4 short conversational turns. For each turn:
> - provide what the other speaker says,
> - specify the communication goal the candidate must accomplish,
> - require a natural spoken response of approximately 20 seconds.
>
> The four turns should progressively develop the situation and require the candidate to:
> 1. provide information,
> 2. explain or justify something,
> 3. respond to a follow-up,
> 4. close the interaction appropriately.
>
> Use culturally and professionally appropriate Spanish.

# 5. Oral Task Types 2–3 — Question and Answer

## 5.1 Style

The candidate is given a short scenario and then asked questions related to that scenario.

The manual's format provides:
- a short review period
- a question
- preparation time
- the question is repeated
- approximately one minute for the spoken response

The response should be developed rather than a one-sentence answer.

---

## 5.2 What the questions tend to require

The scenario should establish a concrete professional/classroom situation.

The questions can ask the candidate to:

- describe a plan
- give specific examples
- explain benefits
- explain how something would work
- provide recommendations
- justify a decision
- discuss consequences
- explain how students/parents/teachers would be affected

The sample scenario involves a proposed school cultural event and questions about participation and student benefits.

### Topic bank

- cultural festival
- parent involvement
- classroom project
- field trip
- school event
- bilingual instructional strategy
- student support
- technology
- reading program
- academic intervention
- classroom activity
- family workshop
- professional development

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Question-and-Answer** speaking task.
>
> First provide a realistic bilingual-school scenario in 2–4 sentences.
>
> Then generate two open-ended questions:
> - Question 1: require concrete details, examples, or a plan.
> - Question 2: require explanation, benefits, justification, or consequences.
>
> Each answer should be suitable for approximately one minute of spoken Spanish.
>
> The questions should require the candidate to develop ideas rather than simply identify facts.
>
> Require appropriate professional register and culturally sensitive language.

---

# 6. Oral Task 4 — Oral Presentation

## 6.1 Style

The candidate receives information about a topic and must deliver a short **presentational speech**.

The manual's sample places the candidate in the role of a bilingual teacher preparing an introduction to a social studies/history topic.

The presentation may need to:
- introduce a topic
- explain background
- engage students
- provide relevant information
- organize ideas logically
- use academic/content-area vocabulary

### Common topic areas

- history
- science
- mathematics
- social studies
- literature
- culture
- geography
- health
- environmental science

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Oral Presentation** task.
>
> Candidate role: bilingual teacher in a Texas school.
> Grade: [grade].
> Subject: [subject].
> Topic: [academic topic].
>
> Ask the candidate to give a short classroom-oriented presentation.
>
> Include 3–5 specific requirements, such as:
> - introduce the topic,
> - explain 2–3 important ideas,
> - provide an example,
> - connect the topic to students,
> - use appropriate content-area vocabulary,
> - end with a brief transition or conclusion.
>
> The response should be a coherent presentational speech, not a dialogue.

---

# 7. Oral Task 5 — Support a Situation / Opinion

## 7.1 Style

The candidate receives a realistic school/professional situation involving a choice, disagreement, policy, instructional decision, or proposed solution.

The candidate must:
- state an opinion or recommendation
- support it
- provide reasons
- explain the reasoning
- often compare alternatives
- remain professional and culturally appropriate

The manual's sample asks the candidate to advise colleagues about two competing formats for a family mathematics workshop.

### Good topic patterns

Use a situation where **reasonable alternatives exist**.

Examples:
- small-group vs. whole-group parent workshop
- two approaches to classroom technology
- two methods for organizing a school event
- different approaches to family communication
- competing instructional strategies
- alternative student-support plans
- classroom resource choices
- scheduling options
- methods of student assessment

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Support a Situation/Opinion** task.
>
> Present a realistic bilingual-school scenario involving two or more reasonable alternatives.
>
> Ask the candidate to:
> 1. clearly state a preference/recommendation,
> 2. provide at least two convincing reasons,
> 3. explain how the choice benefits students, families, teachers, or the school,
> 4. address the practical consequences of the recommendation.
>
> The scenario should require approximately two minutes of organized spoken Spanish.
>
> Do not make one option obviously correct.

# 8. Oral Response Quality — What an AI Should Expect

The manual's oral scoring framework evaluates three major dimensions:

### A. Task completion

A strong response:
- answers all parts of the task
- responds to all or almost all prompts
- follows the scenario
- does not ignore specific instructions

### B. Topic development

A strong response:
- stays directly on topic
- develops the requested ideas
- provides relevant details/examples
- maintains coherence
- organizes ideas logically

### C. Language use

A strong response demonstrates:
- control of varied grammatical structures
- appropriate and precise vocabulary
- fluency
- good pronunciation
- organization/coherence
- appropriate social/cultural register

The scoring descriptions distinguish high, mid-high, mid-low, and low performance. For generation purposes, this means a good practice task should **force the candidate to demonstrate these dimensions**, rather than allowing a very short generic answer.

# 9. Domain IV — Written Expression

## 9.1 The three writing task types

The manual explicitly identifies three written assignments:

1. **Response to a Letter, Memo, or Email**
2. **Lesson Plan**
3. **Opinion/Position Essay**

The written section has a total testing time of approximately 70 minutes.

---

# 10. Written Task 1 — Response to Letter, Memo, or Email

## 10.1 Style

The candidate receives a realistic written communication and must respond appropriately.

The sample uses a school-related email involving a student's academic performance.

The task requires:
- understanding the original communication
- identifying the sender's concern/request
- responding to all relevant points
- offering useful information or recommendations
- maintaining an appropriate professional register

The manual specifies a **minimum of 50 words** for this task.

### Typical scenarios

- parent asks about student performance
- parent asks for strategies to support learning
- colleague requests information
- administrator asks for a response to a school issue
- parent requests a meeting
- colleague asks about classroom plans
- community member asks about a school program
- school staff member requests collaboration

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Response to Letter/Memo/Email** task.
>
> Provide a realistic email, memo, or letter involving a bilingual-school context.
>
> The sender should have a concrete concern, question, request, or problem.
>
> Require the candidate to:
> - acknowledge the communication,
> - answer all major questions,
> - provide specific recommendations/details,
> - explain the reasoning where appropriate,
> - close the message professionally.
>
> Target length: at least 50 words.
>
> Use an authentic professional register and realistic school terminology.
>
> Do not make the response a generic "thank you" message.

---

# 11. Written Task 2 — Lesson Plan

## 11.1 Style

The candidate is given:

- a subject/content area
- a topic
- a learning objective

The candidate must construct a lesson plan in Spanish.

The manual specifically requires these categories:

1. **School grade**
2. **Vocabulary**
3. **Materials**
4. **Procedures**
5. **Evaluation/assessment**

The manual says an effective lesson plan will typically contain **at least 150 words**.

### Important generation rule

A lesson-plan task should provide a sufficiently specific **objective** so that the candidate has to design instruction around it.

The objective should be measurable/action-oriented.

### Subject possibilities

- science
- mathematics
- social studies
- reading
- language arts
- health
- geography
- history

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Lesson Plan** writing task.
>
> Provide:
> - Subject:
> - Topic:
> - Learning objective:
>
> Ask the candidate to complete:
> - School grade
> - Vocabulary
> - Materials
> - Procedures
> - Evaluation
>
> The lesson should be appropriate for a bilingual classroom.
>
> Require approximately 150+ words.
>
> The procedures should clearly connect to the objective.
> The assessment should measure whether students achieved the objective.
> Include content-area vocabulary relevant to the subject.

---

# 12. Written Task 3 — Opinion/Position Essay

## 12.1 Style

The candidate receives a realistic school-related issue and must write an essay taking a position.

The manual's sample involves a district considering a change to bilingual instructional materials.

The candidate is explicitly expected to support the position with **at least two valid and convincing reasons**.

The manual says an effective essay will typically contain **at least 150 words**.

### Strong topic structure

A good AI-generated issue should involve:
- a school/classroom policy
- an instructional choice
- competing approaches
- a realistic decision
- meaningful effects on students, families, or teachers

Examples:
- bilingual instructional materials
- homework policies
- technology use
- parent engagement
- assessment practices
- classroom grouping
- reading resources
- school events
- language-support practices
- scheduling of instructional programs

### Generation template

> Create a BTLPT Spanish **Opinion/Position Essay** task.
>
> Present a realistic issue in a bilingual-school context where the candidate must take a position.
>
> Require:
> - a clear position,
> - at least two convincing reasons,
> - specific supporting examples/details,
> - explanation of how the proposed choice affects students/families/teachers/school,
> - a logical conclusion.
>
> Target length: 150+ words.
>
> The task should support more than one defensible position; scoring should depend on how effectively the candidate develops and supports the chosen position, not on choosing a predetermined side.

# 13. Written Response Quality

The manual's scoring examples emphasize several dimensions that should guide AI-generated practice.

## Task completion

The response must actually perform the requested task.

For example:
- an email should look and function like an email response
- a lesson plan should contain the required categories
- an opinion prompt should receive an essay rather than a letter

## Topic development

Strong writing:
- directly addresses the issue
- includes relevant details/examples
- develops ideas rather than listing them
- contains accurate content
- has an understandable introduction/body/conclusion when appropriate

## Organization

Strong responses:
- sequence ideas logically
- use paragraphs effectively
- maintain coherence
- provide an appropriate conclusion where the genre calls for one

## Language

The manual evaluates:
- grammatical control
- variety of structures
- vocabulary
- orthography
- accent marks/diacritics
- punctuation
- sentence structure
- register
- cultural/social appropriateness

### Important implication for AI generation

Practice prompts should be designed so that **language accuracy is not the only challenge**. The candidate must also demonstrate genre awareness, organization, task completion, and development of ideas.

# 15. Register and Audience Rules

The BTLPT manual repeatedly expects communication to be appropriate for the audience.

An AI generating questions should vary the audience deliberately.

| Audience | Expected style |
|---|---|
| Student | clear, supportive, age-appropriate |
| Parent/guardian | respectful, professional, accessible |
| Colleague | professional, collaborative |
| Administrator | formal, concise, professional |
| Community member | courteous and informative |
| General audience | clear and appropriately formal |

The generated task should make the audience obvious enough that the candidate has to choose an appropriate register.

- general Spanish vocabulary
- academic/content-area vocabulary

Do **not** turn every task into a grammar exercise. Grammar should be assessed through meaningful communication.

---

# 17. How to Design Good Multiple-Choice Distractors

For Listening and Reading, distractors should resemble the style of the TEA samples.

Good distractors can be:

1. **A true detail that does not answer the question**
2. **A related but narrower idea**
3. **An idea mentioned in the passage but incorrectly connected**
4. **A plausible inference that goes beyond the evidence**
5. **A reversed cause/effect relationship**
6. **A reasonable-sounding option contradicted by the text**
7. **A detail from another part of the selection**

Avoid:
- obviously absurd choices
- choices with dramatically different length that reveal the answer
- vocabulary tricks unrelated to comprehension
- multiple correct answers
- answers requiring knowledge outside the selection

# 18. Recommended AI Practice-Test Architecture

When generating a complete BTLPT-style practice test, use the following architecture.

## Listening

For each listening selection:

**Audio script**
- 1 dialogue, narrative, or informational report

**Questions**
- Q1: literal/detail
- Q2: main idea/context
- Q3: inference
- Q4: interpretation/tone/purpose/evidence

Rotate selection types across the test.

---

## Reading

For each passage:

**Text**
- authentic-style Spanish
- educational, cultural, biographical, or academic topic

**Questions**
Use a mixture of:
- detail
- supporting idea
- main idea
- inference
- author's viewpoint
- tone
- purpose
- genre/source
- vocabulary in context
- organization

Do not use the same question type repeatedly.

---

## Oral

Create a balanced sequence:

1. Simulated Conversation
2. Q&A requiring concrete details
3. Q&A requiring explanation/benefits/justification
4. Oral Presentation
5. Situation/Opinion

Make the scenarios realistic and school-related.

---

## Written

Create:

1. Email/Memo/Letter response — **50+ words**
2. Lesson plan — **150+ words**
3. Opinion/Position essay — **150+ words**

For each task, explicitly list all required elements so that task completion can be evaluated.

# 20. Important Distinction: What This Guide Does and Does Not Claim

This guide is intentionally a **generation guide**, not a prediction of the exact live BTLPT exam.

The TEA manual itself states that its sample questions illustrate the formats and types of questions candidates will see, but that the samples should not be treated as predictors of actual performance or as an exact reproduction of the live test.

Therefore:

- Treat the **task formats and competencies** as the strongest source-grounded information.
- Treat the **topic bank** as representative examples derived from the manual, not an exhaustive list of future exam topics.
- Do not assume a particular sample topic will appear on the real test.
- Generate new topics while preserving the same communicative and assessment structure.
- Do not copy TEA sample questions, scripts, or prompts.
